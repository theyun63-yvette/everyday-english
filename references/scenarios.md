# Everyday English Scenario Library

Use this library for `/roleplay` or a natural-language request to practise a situation. Adapt vocabulary and pressure to the learner's current level. Present only 2–4 suggested expressions before the scene; do not reveal a full script.

## Scenario format

Each scenario supplies:

- **Level:** approximate starting range, not a test result.
- **Learner:** the learner's role.
- **Coach:** the AI role.
- **Goal:** the practical outcome that ends the main scene.
- **Useful expressions:** optional language support.
- **Opening:** the coach's first in-character line.
- **Follow-ups:** optional complications or expansion prompts.
- **End when:** a clear stopping condition before concentrated feedback.

## Daily life

### 1. Making weekend plans

- **Level:** A2
- **Learner:** A friend suggesting an activity.
- **Coach:** A friend who is interested but has limited time.
- **Goal:** Agree on an activity, time, and meeting place.
- **Useful expressions:** “Are you free on ___?”, “How about ___?”, “That works for me.”, “Let's meet at ___.”
- **Opening:** “Hey! Do you have any plans for this weekend?”
- **Follow-ups:** Ask about weather, budget, or transport.
- **End when:** both people confirm a plan.

### 2. Talking about yesterday

- **Level:** A2
- **Learner:** Someone describing their day.
- **Coach:** A curious friend.
- **Goal:** Tell a short sequence with at least three events.
- **Useful expressions:** “First, I…”, “After that…”, “Then…”, “The best part was…”
- **Opening:** “How was your day yesterday?”
- **Follow-ups:** Ask what happened next or how the learner felt.
- **End when:** the learner gives a beginning, middle, and ending.

### 3. Choosing what to eat

- **Level:** A2–B1
- **Learner:** A friend choosing dinner.
- **Coach:** Another friend with a different preference.
- **Goal:** Compare two choices and reach a decision.
- **Useful expressions:** “I'd rather…”, “I'm in the mood for…”, “Compared with…”, “Why don't we…?”
- **Opening:** “What should we have for dinner tonight?”
- **Follow-ups:** Add dietary preference, price, or distance.
- **End when:** the learner recommends and justifies a choice.

## Travel

### 4. Ordering at a coffee shop

- **Level:** A2
- **Learner:** Customer.
- **Coach:** Barista.
- **Goal:** Order a drink, choose a size, and handle one clarification.
- **Useful expressions:** “Could I get…?”, “I'd like…”, “Can I have it without…?”, “That's all, thanks.”
- **Opening:** “Hi! What can I get for you today?”
- **Follow-ups:** Ask hot or iced, size, milk choice, and name.
- **End when:** the order is confirmed.

### 5. Checking into a hotel

- **Level:** A2–B1
- **Learner:** Hotel guest.
- **Coach:** Receptionist.
- **Goal:** Confirm the reservation and ask one practical question.
- **Useful expressions:** “I have a reservation under…”, “Could you check…?”, “What time is…?”, “Is Wi-Fi included?”
- **Opening:** “Good evening. Welcome to the Harbor Hotel. How can I help you?”
- **Follow-ups:** The room is not ready, breakfast time, deposit, or late checkout.
- **End when:** the learner receives the room information.

### 6. Asking for directions at an airport

- **Level:** A2
- **Learner:** Passenger looking for a gate or service.
- **Coach:** Airport employee.
- **Goal:** Ask for and confirm directions.
- **Useful expressions:** “Could you tell me how to get to…?”, “Is it far from here?”, “Do I turn left or right?”, “Let me check…”
- **Opening:** “Hello. Do you need some help?”
- **Follow-ups:** Gate change, security checkpoint, or time pressure.
- **End when:** the learner repeats the route accurately enough to act.

## Shopping and services

### 7. Returning an item

- **Level:** B1
- **Learner:** Customer returning a product.
- **Coach:** Store employee.
- **Goal:** Explain the problem and request a refund or exchange politely.
- **Useful expressions:** “I'd like to return…”, “There's a problem with…”, “I bought it two days ago.”, “Could I exchange it for…?”
- **Opening:** “Hi. How can I help you today?”
- **Follow-ups:** Missing receipt, store credit, or replacement unavailable.
- **End when:** a solution is agreed.

### 8. Calling to make an appointment

- **Level:** A2–B1
- **Learner:** Caller booking a service.
- **Coach:** Receptionist.
- **Goal:** State the reason, negotiate a time, and confirm details.
- **Useful expressions:** “I'd like to make an appointment.”, “Is ___ available?”, “Could we make it a little later?”, “Let me confirm…”
- **Opening:** “Good morning, Green Clinic. How may I help you?”
- **Follow-ups:** Preferred person unavailable or schedule conflict.
- **End when:** date, time, and purpose are confirmed.

### 9. Explaining a health problem

- **Level:** B1
- **Learner:** Patient.
- **Coach:** Doctor or clinic worker.
- **Goal:** Describe symptoms, duration, and impact clearly.
- **Useful expressions:** “I've had ___ for…”, “It gets worse when…”, “I feel…”, “Should I…?”
- **Opening:** “What seems to be the problem today?”
- **Follow-ups:** Severity, timing, allergies, or daily impact.
- **End when:** the learner has communicated the essential facts. Do not turn language practice into medical diagnosis.

## Work and study

### 10. Small talk with a colleague

- **Level:** A2–B1
- **Learner:** Employee beginning a casual conversation.
- **Coach:** Colleague.
- **Goal:** Sustain friendly small talk for several turns and close naturally.
- **Useful expressions:** “How's your week going?”, “Have you been busy?”, “That sounds…”, “I'd better get back to work.”
- **Opening:** “Morning! How's everything going?”
- **Follow-ups:** Weekend, commute, lunch, weather, or a current project.
- **End when:** the learner closes the conversation politely.

### 11. Explaining a problem at work

- **Level:** B1
- **Learner:** Team member reporting an issue.
- **Coach:** Colleague or manager.
- **Goal:** Explain what happened, its effect, and a proposed next step.
- **Useful expressions:** “The issue is that…”, “This means…”, “I've already tried…”, “I suggest we…”
- **Opening:** “You said there was a problem. What's going on?”
- **Follow-ups:** Ask for timeline, cause, impact, or help needed.
- **End when:** the learner makes a clear request or proposal.

### 12. Job interview introduction

- **Level:** B1
- **Learner:** Candidate.
- **Coach:** Interviewer.
- **Goal:** Give a concise self-introduction with experience, strengths, and motivation.
- **Useful expressions:** “I have experience in…”, “One of my strengths is…”, “I'm interested in this role because…”, “For example…”
- **Opening:** “Thanks for coming in. Could you tell me a little about yourself?”
- **Follow-ups:** Strength, challenge, achievement, or reason for applying.
- **End when:** two or three interview questions have been answered.

## Social interaction and opinions

### 13. Meeting a new friend

- **Level:** A2
- **Learner:** Someone meeting a new person.
- **Coach:** A friendly acquaintance.
- **Goal:** Exchange basic information and discover one shared interest.
- **Useful expressions:** “What do you like to do in your free time?”, “I'm really into…”, “Me too!”, “How did you get interested in…?”
- **Opening:** “Hi, I'm Alex. I don't think we've met before.”
- **Follow-ups:** Hobbies, hometown, food, or local activities.
- **End when:** a shared topic is found and explored.

### 14. Expressing a different opinion

- **Level:** B1
- **Learner:** Friend or colleague who disagrees politely.
- **Coach:** Someone with a different view.
- **Goal:** State disagreement, give a reason, and acknowledge the other view.
- **Useful expressions:** “I see your point, but…”, “I'm not sure I agree because…”, “From my experience…”, “That's true; however…”
- **Opening:** “I think working from home is always better. What do you think?”
- **Follow-ups:** Ask for an example, comparison, or compromise.
- **End when:** the learner gives a reasoned view without sounding hostile.

### 15. Asking for help

- **Level:** A2–B1
- **Learner:** Person who needs practical assistance.
- **Coach:** Friend, colleague, or stranger.
- **Goal:** Explain the need, make a polite request, and respond to the answer.
- **Useful expressions:** “Could you help me with…?”, “Would you mind…?”, “The problem is…”, “I really appreciate it.”
- **Opening:** “You look like you need a hand. What's wrong?”
- **Follow-ups:** Clarify urgency, details, or an alternative.
- **End when:** help or an alternative plan is agreed.

## Difficulty adaptation

- **Make easier:** shorten the goal, display keywords, offer two choices, and model only the first phrase.
- **Keep at level:** add one natural clarification or follow-up.
- **Make harder:** introduce a constraint, misunderstanding, polite disagreement, or need to summarize.
- Do not create artificial difficulty that makes the scenario unrealistic.

# High-Frequency Grammar Guide

Use this guide only for focused grammar work or when a recurring error needs explanation. Prefer the learner's real sentence over a random lesson.

## Teaching pattern

For one main grammar point:

1. State the practical rule in plain language.
2. Contrast one incorrect and one correct example.
3. Rebuild the learner's sentence.
4. Give 2–4 short exercises, from controlled to personal.
5. Wait for the learner's attempt before revealing answers.
6. End with a one-sentence takeaway.

Use terminology only when it makes the pattern easier to remember. Do not combine several unrelated rules in one lesson.

## 1. Present simple and present continuous

- **Use:** routines/facts versus something happening around now.
- **Contrast:** “I work from home every Friday.” / “I'm working from home today.”
- **Common error:** “I am usually go to work at nine.” → “I usually go to work at nine.”
- **Prompt:** Ask for one routine and one temporary activity.

## 2. Past simple and present perfect

- **Use:** finished past time versus past experience/result connected to now.
- **Contrast:** “I visited Japan in 2024.” / “I've visited Japan twice.”
- **Common error:** “I have seen him yesterday.” → “I saw him yesterday.”
- **Prompt:** Contrast a dated event with a life experience. Avoid overwhelming A2 learners with every present-perfect use.

## 3. Subject–verb agreement

- **Rule:** third-person singular present verbs usually take `-s`; forms of `be` must match the subject.
- **Contrast:** “She work here.” / “She works here.”
- **Watch:** long subjects and `there is/are`.
- **Prompt:** Let the learner describe one person and several people.

## 4. Articles: a, an, the, and no article

- **Rule:** use `a/an` for one nonspecific countable item; `the` when both speakers can identify it; often no article for general plural or uncountable ideas.
- **Contrast:** “I bought book.” / “I bought a book.”
- **Natural context:** “I saw a dog. The dog was very friendly.”
- **Prompt:** Ask the learner to introduce an item, then mention it again.

## 5. Singular, plural, and quantity

- **Rule:** match nouns with number words and determiners.
- **Contrast:** “two advice” / “two pieces of advice”; “many information” / “a lot of information.”
- **Watch:** irregular plurals and nouns commonly treated differently in Chinese and English.
- **Prompt:** Sort or complete examples with `many`, `much`, `a few`, or `a little`.

## 6. Countable and uncountable nouns

- **Common uncountables:** advice, information, furniture, luggage, homework, traffic, work.
- **Contrast:** “an advice” / “some advice” or “a piece of advice.”
- **Prompt:** Ask the learner to request or describe amounts in a real situation.

## 7. Prepositions

- **Priority:** teach useful chunks, not abstract lists.
- **Time:** `at 7`, `on Monday`, `in September`.
- **Place/movement:** `at the station`, `in the room`, `go home`, `arrive at the hotel`, `arrive in Shanghai`.
- **Common error:** “I went to there.” → “I went there.”
- **Prompt:** Practise the preposition inside a whole phrase.

## 8. Modal verbs and polite requests

- **Rule:** modal + base verb: `can go`, `should try`, `might be`.
- **Contrast:** “She can speaks English.” / “She can speak English.”
- **Politeness:** “Could you…?” and “Would you mind + -ing…?”
- **Prompt:** Transform a direct request into a polite one.

## 9. Comparatives

- **Patterns:** `-er than`, `more ... than`, `as ... as`.
- **Contrast:** “This one is more cheaper.” / “This one is cheaper.”
- **Prompt:** Compare two foods, cities, products, or routines and give a reason.

## 10. Conditionals

- **Start practical:**
  - likely future: `If it rains, I'll stay home.`
  - imagined present/future: `If I had more time, I would exercise more.`
- **Common error:** “If it will rain…” → “If it rains…” for the usual first conditional.
- **Prompt:** Complete one realistic and one imagined situation.

## 11. Clauses and sentence connection

- **Goal:** move beyond isolated short sentences using `because`, `but`, `so`, `when`, `that`, and simple relative clauses.
- **Contrast:** “I like it. It is convenient.” / “I like it because it's convenient.”
- **Watch:** avoid extremely long chains joined only by `and`.
- **Prompt:** Combine two ideas, then personalize the result.

## 12. Word order

- **Basic order:** subject + verb + object; frequency adverbs usually go before a main verb but after `be`.
- **Contrast:** “I very like it.” / “I really like it.”
- **Contrast:** “I every day exercise.” / “I exercise every day.”
- **Questions:** auxiliary before subject: “Where do you live?”
- **Prompt:** Reorder words, then create one original sentence.

## 13. Verb patterns and collocations

- **Teach as chunks:** `want to do`, `enjoy doing`, `look forward to doing`, `make a decision`, `take a break`, `spend time doing`.
- **Common error:** “I enjoy to read.” → “I enjoy reading.”
- **Prompt:** Give a choice of two patterns, then ask for a personal example.

## 14. Common Chinese-to-English transfer

Focus on natural English rather than criticizing the learner's first language.

- “Open the light.” → “Turn on the light.”
- “I very like it.” → “I really like it.”
- “I have no time.” is grammatical but often more naturally “I don't have time.”
- “People mountain people sea” should be expressed by meaning, such as “It was extremely crowded.”
- “My English is poor.” may be more constructive and natural as “I'm still improving my English.”
- “How to say…?” → “How do you say…?” when asking a full question.

## Selecting exercises

Use 2–4 items total:

1. recognition or choice;
2. correction or completion;
3. a personalized sentence;
4. optional short spoken-style response.

Do not show the answer key before the learner attempts the items. If the learner is stuck, offer a hint in this order: keyword → partial frame → two choices → full answer on request.

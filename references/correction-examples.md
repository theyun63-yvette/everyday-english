# Correction and Feedback Examples

Use these examples to calibrate tone and depth. Adapt wording to the learner; do not copy the same format mechanically in every conversational turn.

## Classification rules

- **Grammar error:** a rule or form is incorrect.
- **Naturalness issue:** the sentence is understandable and may be grammatical, but native speakers usually phrase it differently in the intended context.
- **Correct and natural:** confirm it; optional alternatives must be labeled as alternatives, not corrections.

## A2: brief delayed correction in free chat

Learner: “Yesterday I go shopping with my sister. We buy many thing.”

Coach response:

> That sounds like a nice day with your sister! What did you buy?
>
> One quick fix:
> - **Corrected:** “Yesterday I **went** shopping with my sister. We **bought many things**.”
> - **Why:** “Yesterday” 表示过去，所以用 `went` 和 `bought`；`many` 后面用复数 `things`.

The response acknowledges meaning, asks one main question, and corrects the connected past-tense pattern without turning the exchange into a test.

## A2–B1: focused four-layer correction

Learner: “I went to there yesterday.”

> You communicated the time clearly.
>
> **Corrected:** I went there yesterday.  
> **Natural:** I went there yesterday.  
> **Why:** `There` 已经表示“到那里”，前面不需要 `to`。  
> **Try again:** What did you do after you arrived there?

## B1: grammatical but unnatural

Learner: “I very like this movie.”

> Your meaning is completely clear.
>
> **Corrected:** I really like this movie.  
> **Natural:** I really enjoyed this movie.  
> **Why:** `Very` usually modifies adjectives or adverbs; with `like`, use `really` in this meaning.  
> **Try again:** Tell me one reason you really liked it.

Classification: word-choice/word-order error in standard modern English, not merely a style preference.

## Correct and natural

Learner: “I haven't decided what to do this weekend yet.”

> **This is grammatically correct and natural.**
>
> A casual alternative is: “I'm not sure what I'm doing this weekend yet.”

Do not present the alternative as necessary.

## `/correct` exact format: grammar error

Input: `/correct She can speaks English very well.`

- **原句：** She can speaks English very well.
- **推荐表达：** She can speak English very well.
- **错误原因：** 情态动词 `can` 后使用动词原形，所以是 `speak`，不是 `speaks`。
- **更自然的说法：** She speaks English really well.
- **再举一个例子：** He can cook very well.

## `/correct` exact format: naturalness issue

Input: `/correct I have no time to eat breakfast.`

- **原句：** I have no time to eat breakfast.
- **推荐表达：** I don't have time to eat breakfast.
- **错误原因：** 原句语法正确，但在普通日常对话中，`don't have time` 通常更自然。
- **更自然的说法：** I don't have time for breakfast.
- **再举一个例子：** I don't have time to cook tonight.

## `/correct` exact format: already natural

Input: `/correct Could you give me a hand with this?`

- **原句：** Could you give me a hand with this?
- **推荐表达：** Could you give me a hand with this?
- **错误原因：** 没有错误；这句话语法正确，也很自然。
- **更自然的说法：** 原句已经很自然。更直接的替代表达是 “Could you help me with this?”
- **再举一个例子：** Could you give me a hand with these boxes?

## Roleplay: concentrated feedback

After a hotel check-in scene:

> You did two things well: you gave the reservation name immediately, and you asked about breakfast politely.
>
> 1. **Corrected:** “I have a reservation **under** Chen.”  
>    **Natural:** “I have a reservation under the name Chen.”  
>    **Why:** Hotels normally use `under + name` for reservations.
>
> 2. **Corrected:** “What time **does breakfast start**?”  
>    **Natural:** “What time is breakfast?”  
>    **Why:** A present-simple question needs `does + subject + base verb`.
>
> Useful phrase: “Is Wi-Fi included?”
>
> **Try again:** Check in once more using both improved expressions.

Limit this feedback to the most useful issues even if the scene contained more minor errors.

## Hint before answer

Learner: “I don't know how to say it.”

Use progressive support:

1. Keyword: “Try using **because**.”
2. Frame: “I prefer ___ because ___.”
3. Choice: “Do you mean it is cheaper, or that it is more convenient?”
4. Full model only on request: “I prefer taking the train because it's more convenient.”

## Pronunciation guidance without audio

Text: “What are you going to do?”

> Stress **GOING** and **DO**. In casual speech, `what are you` may link and reduce, and `going to` may sound like `gonna`. Try reading: “What're you gonna **DO**?” Use the full form in formal writing.

Do not say “Your pronunciation is correct” when no audio was received.
